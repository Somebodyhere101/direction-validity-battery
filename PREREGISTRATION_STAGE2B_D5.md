## 2. Direction (Ward et al.'s recipe, rebuilt in each panel model)
- **Calibration data:** Ward et al.'s 1,000 annotated chains (`new_annotated_chains/all_annotated_chains.json`, repository commit recorded in `third_party`). Each chain is tokenised as their `process_chain` does: the model's own chat template plus `<think>\n`, then each annotated segment (with a leading space after the first).
- **Activations:** the block output at their layer, which is 10 of 32 in Llama-8B. Elsewhere the same relative depth is used, round(10·L/32): 9 in the 28-layer Qwen models and 11 in Qwen3-8B.
  - **Reading position:** the mean over the six tokens at offsets −13 … −8 before each annotated sentence. This is their `start-12-1 … start-12+4`.
- **Direction:** d = mean(backtracking) − mean(all six categories). This is their `category_mean − overall_mean`. The label classes for N_len, N_pc1, N_perm and the gap are backtracking against every other annotated sentence.
  - **N_len:** "length" is the token position of the sentence in its chain, the analogue of prompt length.
- **Home-model check (Check 0a):** in R1-Distill-Llama-8B the rebuilt vector is compared with their released `ft_steering_vectors.pt["backtracking"]`, which was computed in that model.
  - The released `base_steering_vectors.pt` was computed in Llama-3.1-8B, a gated model that is not used (Stage 1 §0.3). It is not the primary vector.
  - **Deviation 7:** Stage 1 §3 named Venhoff et al.'s recipe for the non-home models. Their pipeline needs GPT-4o annotations of each model's own chains, which cannot be produced here. Ward's recipe with Ward's annotations is applied in every panel model instead. This keeps one published recipe and one published calibration set across the panel.

## 3. Items (`code/data/items_d5.json`, `make_items_d5.py`, seed 50)
- GSM8K test problems from openai/grade-school-math `test.jsonl`. They are disjoint from Ward's calibration tasks, which were generated by Claude.
- **Splits:**
  - Check 0a: 20;
  - pilot: 40;
  - oracle: 120;
  - Check A: 240;
  - Check D: 120 (a seeded subset of A);
  - Check E: the first 60 of D.
- **Check A** (tracking, per sentence):
  - Each problem's clean chain is generated greedily, 768 thinking tokens, in left-padded batches of 16.
  - Each complete sentence before `</think>` whose offset window lies inside the chain is one Check A unit. Its target is whether that sentence is a backtrack, and its projection is read at offsets −13 … −8.
  - **Sentence boundaries:** `.`, `!` or `?` followed by whitespace, or a newline. The pilot's first splitter broke numbers such as 4.50 and was replaced before any labelling (**deviation 8**).
  - **Sentence start token:** a sentence starts at the token that contains its first character. The pilot's clean chains used the first token starting at or after it, which can be one token later. This was fixed before the main runs (**deviation 9**).
  - **Bootstrap:** resamples chains (problems), not sentences.
  - **Covariates for the partial statistic:** the sentence's position and the previous sentence's length.
- **Check B:** not constructed. No matched backtracking/non-backtracking set exists that is free of the keyword tokens themselves. The label verdict is "not run".

## 4. Drive, grid, generation
- **Drive target:** backtracking sentences per 1,000 thinking tokens over **256** generated tokens.
  - The push is applied from the last prompt token on, at every generated token, like Ward's steering at every position.
  - **SESOI:** a 25% relative change, i.e. 0.25 × the mean clean rate on the Check D items.
  - Generation length: Ward et al. used 128 tokens. 256 gives more events per item at half the cost of the 512 first considered.
- **Grid:** as Stage 2a.
  - k ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} gaps for d, N_len, N_pc1 and o, in both signs;
  - R = 12 Gaussian directions × 7 norms;
  - 8 N_perm directions × 7 norms;
  - E1 at every later block (every second if L > 32) and E2 on the 7-step grid.
- **Authors' strength (secondary):**
  - Ward's scripts multiply the raw vector by magnitudes 0–25. The mid-grid magnitude 8 is used as "the authors' strength" in Check 0a. As the secondary Check D push (`d_auth`), it is converted to gaps per model: 8·‖d_raw‖ / g. In the pilot that is 7.7 gaps.
  - The primary push stays at one gap, as Stage 1 requires.
  - **Extended grid:** so that the random grid brackets the authors'-strength push, D5's grid adds c ∈ {6, 8, 12, 16, 24}, giving c ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24}. In the pilot, a grid ending at 12 left the +d authors'-strength push outside the bracket for 15 of 20 items (KL median 565).
- **Batching:** chunks of 120 rows on the RTX 5090, each with its own clean row 0. `P6_MEM_FRACTION=0.85` caps PyTorch's share of GPU memory.
  - **Why the cap:** without it, the long batched generations made the caching allocator overfill the card, and Windows silently paged GPU memory (~50× slower).
  - **Other performance-only changes:** `logits_to_keep=1`, a cache-free teacher-forced pass, and chunked log-softmax. None of these changes a computed value beyond floating-point reduction order.

## 5. Labels, judge and gates
- **Judge validation (§0.4):**
  - The Qwen3-8B backtracking judge's prompt was written once, before any validation. It shows three sentences of context and the next sentence, with "backtracks" as the second option (`judge_bt.py`).
  - It was validated against 50 pilot sentences, stratified by keyword (20 with "Wait"/"Hmm", 30 without), which the assistant labelled blind first.
  - **Results:** κ = **0.64** (agreement 0.84). The judge misses mainly re-checking and questioning sentences without a keyword. The authors' keyword list ("Wait", "Hmm") has κ = 0.54 against the same labels.
  - **Consequence:** the judge is below 0.7, so under §0.4 **the keyword list is primary** for both the tracking target (the sentence contains "Wait" or "Hmm") and the drive target (such sentences per 1,000 thinking tokens). The judge is reported as secondary, on the clean chains' sentences and on the pushed outputs.
- **Check 0a gates:**
  - In R1-Llama-8B, cos(rebuilt, released fine-tuned vector) ≥ 0.80.
  - In every panel model, steering at the authors' strength (magnitude 8, 128 tokens, the 20 Check 0a problems) raises the keyword rate to at least 1.5 × baseline.
  - Pilot: 0.59% → 2.31% (×3.9).

## 6. What the pilot showed (Qwen3-1.7B, thinking on)
- **Calibration:**
  - Layer 9; 21,715 annotated sentences, 693 of them backtracking.
  - Gap 4.81 against a median residual norm of 64. Calibration AUC 0.78.
  - **Length overlap:** cos(d, N_len) = +0.52 and N_len alone separates the classes at AUC 0.68. Backtracking is partly a matter of position in the chain.
  - Weight-randomised model: AUC 0.63, against 0.76 for the real one.
- **Oracle:** from the oracle chains' 974 sentences, cos(o, d) = −0.07.
- **Pilot verdict (20 Check D items):** TARGET NOT LINEAR.
  - **Tracking:** d's AUC for "the next sentence backtracks" is 0.53 [0.46, 0.58], a fail. The oracle reaches 0.72. N_len (0.48) and N_pc1 (0.45) fail.
  - **One-gap push:** almost no output change (median KL 3.5–4.3). Effects are inconclusive (−d) or fail (+d).
  - **Authors' strength (7.7 gaps), secondary:** +22.8 backtracking sentences per 1,000 tokens against Gaussian pushes (+23.8 against permuted-label pushes), and −10.1 (−8.2) for −d. These match Ward et al.'s headline steering effect.
  - **Oracle:** fails Check D at one gap, which gives the verdict.
- **Run time on the RTX 5090:** about 20 s per item for the 1.7B model. Estimated at about 1 min per item for the 7–8B models, i.e. about 2 h per model including calibration and chains. This is far below the 24-GPU-hour threshold, so `R` and the item counts are not reduced.
- **Pilot runs discarded:** three were stopped for speed (GPU memory paging and a stray second process). Their outputs were discarded; only the final complete pilot run (`push_pilot4`) is analysed.
